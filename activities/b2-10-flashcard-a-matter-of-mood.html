The expression: feel like

Use feel like to express what you want or what you are in the mood for.

FEEL LIKE + noun / -ING
Noun“I feel like some pizza.”
Verb + -ing“I feel like going for a walk.”

Notice: “I feel like going out” — not “I feel like to go out”.

A preference is also a conversation

These responses are about communicating naturally, not reproducing a prescribed sentence. Write your own answer, then compare it with a model.

Situation 1 · Suggest something

After class, a colleague suggests getting something to eat. You agree, but have a particular kind of food in mind. Express your preference using feel like + noun.

Situation 2 · Decline tactfully

A friend suggests going out tonight. You would rather have a quiet evening, but you want to suggest another day. Use don't feel like + -ing without sounding abrupt.

Your answers stay on this device.

Possible responses — not the only correct ones

1. “That sounds lovely. I feel like something Italian, if that works for you.”

2. “I don't really feel like going out tonight. Could we arrange something for tomorrow instead?”

Check your choices: Did you use a noun phrase in the first response and an -ing form in the second? Did your second response keep the conversation open?
